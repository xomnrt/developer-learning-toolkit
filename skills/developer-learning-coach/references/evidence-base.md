# Evidence map and claim boundaries

Use this as a source map, not as a script. Prefer the original paper, a
systematic review, an official publisher page, or an established research
institution. Cite the source near a factual claim when the final answer relies
on it.

## Motivation and perceived competence

Self-determination theory distinguishes autonomy, competence, and relatedness
as needs relevant to motivation. Use this to form a hypothesis about conditions
that support or undermine engagement, not to infer a fixed personality trait.

- Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000), *Self-Determination Theory and the
  Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being*:
  https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68
- APA overview of the research program:
  https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory

## Retrieval and spacing

Retrieval practice can improve later retention more than repeated study in the
tested settings. Distributed practice has broad support, while the most useful
interval depends on the desired retention interval and material. Do not present
one universal review schedule as research-mandated.

- Karpicke, J. D., & Roediger, H. L. (2008), *The Critical Importance of
  Retrieval for Learning*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/
- Cepeda, N. J., et al. (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A
  Review and Quantitative Synthesis*:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/

## Worked examples, guidance, and fading

Worked examples can reduce unproductive load for novices learning complex
procedures. Their benefit depends on design, prior expertise, and active
processing. Guidance should fade as independent performance improves.

- Sweller, J., & Cooper, G. A. (1985), *The Use of Worked Examples as a
  Substitute for Problem Solving in Learning Algebra*:
  https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3
- Renkl, A., Atkinson, R. K., & Grosse, C. S. (2004), *How Fading Worked
  Solution Steps Works - A Cognitive Load Perspective*:
  https://doi.org/10.1023/B:TRUC.0000021815.74806.F6

These studies do not justify giving every developer a complete solution first.
Choose example-first, guided, or problem-first entry from prior knowledge, task
structure, and the learner's actual response to challenge.

## Productive difficulty and failure

Performance during practice is not identical to durable learning. Spacing,
retrieval, variation, and some delayed feedback can make practice feel harder
while supporting retention or transfer. A difficulty is not desirable merely
because it is hard; the learner needs prerequisite knowledge and feedback.

- Bjork Learning and Forgetting Lab, research overview:
  https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/
- Kapur, M. (2008), *Productive Failure*:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802212669

Do not universalize productive failure. Evidence from a bounded instructional
design does not support leaving a learner indefinitely unsupported in an
arbitrary production task.

## Learning-styles warning

People have preferences and differing abilities, but the evidence reviewed by
Pashler and colleagues did not support prescribing instruction by matching
visual, auditory, or kinesthetic learner labels. Discuss strategies and task
demands instead.

- Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008), *Learning Styles:
  Concepts and Evidence*:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x

## Generalization boundary

Much of this evidence comes from controlled educational tasks rather than
professional software development. Mark adaptations such as timeboxes, session
lengths, difficulty ratios, and project structures as coaching hypotheses to be
tested against the learner's performance. Do not market the resulting profile
as scientifically validated or causally proven.
